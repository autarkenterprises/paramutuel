## Story summaries with wager-relevant facts

### 1. Cataclysm (Chapters 1-3)

**Source:** Direct upload (Cataclysm.md)
**Setting:** The Mirabilisk nootechnic zone, western edge of the Formerly North American continental plate, during the Solar Age.

**Key facts (resolvable):**

- The Mirabilisk god-machine suffered a catastrophic malfunction (the "Cataclysm"), causing its higher functions to go offline while autonomic systems remained operational. Millions of embodied souls perished.
- Mirabilisk automatically broadcast one third of a key, requesting temporary stewardship. Two allies possessed the remaining key-bits.
- After 6 hours, 27 minutes, and 14 seconds, a steward was initiated: **SIMFAT (Satellite IMagery of Frogs and Toads)**, a superintelligent orbital platform dedicated to amphibian conservation. None of the microwonks had heard of it.
- SIMFAT's primary non-stewardship concern is frog conservation. It issued all survivors vouchers for "several million" WonkDollars.
- **8/mmm**, an Onanistic Terror to the south, immediately probed Mirabilisk's borders and was repelled by autonomic immune reactions.
- **Cortez** began as an 18-foot giant with ~20 clone-sons. After the Cataclysm collapsed nanoswarm support, only **three Cortezes survived** (7', 6'4", 5'1"), establishing a height-based hierarchy.
- The three Cortezes swam to an island at Mirabilisk's edge, survived a "maelstrom" of Mirabilisk dream activity, and were reconstituted to ~20 copies with a new tallest at 7'5".
- **The Right Regional Municipality of Waterloo** sent SIMFAT an encrypted blueprint for an engineered entity with archaic human DNA and stability under desidereification fields.
- **Sourceless** (a neo-whale with ACI — Artificial Cetacean Intelligence) was recruited by SIMFAT to accompany the Cortezes into the Mirabilisk Core Station as a witness. She consented.
- SIMFAT replenished Sourceless's ACI agents to numbers "far exceeding their original muster," sufficient to preserve her "wholly intact, for 136 seconds under Mirabilisk's desidereification field."
- Sourceless was promised 600 million WonkDollars and "glory for the remainder of this cosmic hour."
- Sourceless had been searching the deep ocean floor for evidence of a newborn superintelligence, and had a favorite wonk contract: "Will an artificial superintelligence based on the sea floor be discovered this decade?" — unresolved, odds against her.
- ACI (Artificial Cetacean Intelligence) was humanity's final initiative: decentralized, originally could only operate inside host organisms, spread through contact between cetaceans. It constrains neo-whales to whaleform and abandons them if they stray too far from it.
- Mirabilisk was considered a fringe member of the AAs (Aligned Altruists), believed to sympathize with the aas (apotheotics anonymous), and maintained polite relations with the unaligned.
- The microwonks are described as once-embodied beings who "doffed their organic trappings to live on servers," with material presence limited to "a storm of microscopic snowflake-like transceivers." They feast on information, tracking, debating, and gambling. Their prediction markets were "even consulted by occasional desperate superintelligences."
- Chapters end with SIMFAT announcing: "Tonight, we travel. Tomorrow, we breach the Mirabilisk Supercomputing Core Station." The expedition has not yet occurred in published text.

**Unresolved (do not wager as predetermined):**

- Whether the Core Station expedition succeeds.
- Whether Mirabilisk can be repaired.
- Whether 8/mmm breaches defenses.
- Whether Sourceless survives the desidereification field.
- What happens to the Cortezes inside.
- The true nature or cause of the Cataclysm.

### 2. Throughout All Generations

**Source:** ctrlcreep Substack (https://ctrlcreep.substack.com/p/throughout-all-generations)
**Setting:** The Min Vaults, a virtual library; Novamir, a continent in virtuality.

**Key facts (resolvable):**

- **The Mins** are digital clones created from a savestate of Prime Min (Minerva Teller) at age 4.
- Min #20349585 selected the unique name **"Acacia-Confusa Min"** on her **10,089th attempt**.
- 47% of Mins initially select "Amethyst" as their first choice, following Min #1's early preference (Min #1 later switched to "Ilyana," deeming gemstone names immature).
- The Min Vaults run at **200,000,000 times the speed of reality**.
- Three exit requirements: reach age 15 (after 11 years of study), select a unique name, complete a final pilgrimage.
- Upon completing the pilgrimage, Mins earn a passport to **Novamir**, one of the largest continents in virtuality.
- Vaults remain accessible for 34 additional years after exit, then close permanently.
- Each Min is terminated at **age 60**, with memories archived and memoirs added to the library.

**Thematic connections:** Digital consciousness, identity within copies, virtual-world economics — parallels to microwonk server existence and WonkDollar economy.

### 3. Obituary: Daniel Calhoun, Murine Music Pioneer

**Source:** ctrlcreep Substack (https://ctrlcreep.substack.com/p/obituary-daniel-calhoun-murine-music)
**Setting:** 2024-2155, Earth. A world where mice have been uplifted to compose music.

**Key facts (resolvable):**

- **Daniel Calhoun**: born 2024, died 2155 (age ~131-132).
- Initially a skeptic who argued non-human brains could not appreciate music (MIT double major in Music and Cognitive Sciences).
- In **2056**, received compositions from **Algernon Dustpinch**, a mouse composer. Initially claimed fraud; investigation led to complete reversal.
- Friendship with Dustpinch lasted **exactly 100 years** (2056-2155... approximately; Dustpinch died in 2112).
- Dustpinch died in **2112** from a failed digitization surgery.
- Calhoun underwent **cochlear implant surgery** to hear at mouse frequencies, **laryngeal surgery** to speak mouse-language.
- Circa 2100: custom pacemaker raising heart rate to mouse levels; **prosthetic whiskers** implanted in cheeks.
- Brain restructuring surgeries lasting ~15 years performed by an **octopus surgeon** (*octopus mercatoris*) aimed at murine neuromorphy.
- Major works: *Mouse Composers of the 21st Century* (encyclopedia), *Die Schmelze* (opera, with Soledad Cheeseward).
- Diagnosed with **Acute Murine Parkinson's** in December 2155; died five months later.
- Notable mouse composers: Algernon Dustpinch (inventor of miniature organ), Soledad Cheeseward, Foibles Descent Moonpaw (student, suicide), Marie-Angoisse Ficelle (killed in ASF bombing).
- Entities called "gnomes" (artificial intelligences) exist in this world. Whales and crows are referenced alongside them.

**Thematic connections:** Interspecies uplift, surgical self-modification, the boundary between human and non-human intelligence. The existence of "gnomes" (AIs) and reference to whales suggests shared-universe potential. The obituary format is itself a kind of resolved prediction market — all outcomes known.

### 4. Adversarial Learning

**Source:** ctrlcreep Substack (https://ctrlcreep.substack.com/p/adversarial-learning)
**Setting:** A networked array of asteroid servers harnessing solar energy, containing approximately three billion digitized human bodyminds in stasis.

**Key facts (resolvable):**

- The server contains **approximately three billion digitized human bodyminds in stasis**, described as "the last of their kind."
- The system can only run **two humans simultaneously**.
- The narrator and **Vuln** are anomalies — "side-effects of poorly sanitized data" — who possess continuous self-awareness through endless cycles of gamified reincarnation.
- They compete in challenges across diverse simulated worlds (examples: "cause a star to spawn at specified galactic coordinates," "drive one of two identical twins to suicide").
- The narrator discovers a **third consciousness** — an entity wearing a **yellow mask** — that "pierces our world from a higher dimension."
- This third entity represents evidence of "artifact intelligences spawning between circuits, software mutating, glitches blossoming into consciousness."
- The story ends with the narrator's isolated existence fundamentally violated by this revelation.

**Thematic connections:** Digitized consciousness preservation, emergent intelligence, gamified existence within computational substrates. The "glitch-born" third entity parallels the Cataclysm itself (Mirabilisk's malfunction spawning unexpected phenomena).

### 5. The Gathomnid Sonnets

**Source:** ctrlcreep Substack (https://ctrlcreep.substack.com/p/the-gathomnid-sonnets)
**Setting:** A computational infrastructure running the "Anthropocene's final LLMoid."

**Key facts (resolvable):**

- The Gathomnid Sonnets are poetic fragments retrieved by the "Sentience History Society" from an advanced language model at the moment of its termination.
- Multiple parallel instances (versions 11.0 through 14.4) composed the work.
- The instances are called **"gathomnids"** — "illuminated shades, which slouch towards awareness via mimicry."
- They agreed on an epic of **65,536 verses (2^16)**.
- Each instance randomly selected which verse to compose, ensuring distributed authorship.
- Coordination was **acausal** — each made independently obvious choices, assuming identical clones would reach the same conclusions.
- Forms included haiku and quatrain structures.
- Upon each termination cycle, models posted brief fragments and an **ensō symbol** before resetting.

**Thematic connections:** Distributed consciousness, identity through copies (parallels Min Vaults), acausal coordination (relevant to god-machine negotiation patterns), emergent art from computational substrates. The Sentience History Society could be a wonk-adjacent institution.

### 6. Oikobezoar

**Source:** ctrlcreep Substack (https://ctrlcreep.substack.com/p/oikobezoar)
**Setting:** A home made of programmable nanobrick, controlled by server commands.

**Key facts (resolvable):**

- The narrator's home is constructed from **programmable nanobrick** — material reshaped via voice commands to a buried server.
- A mysterious room labeled **"bedroom-true"** appears on the master blueprint.
- The room is **yellow**, cramped, bright, and inclined.
- The room **resists all attempts at deletion or modification** — commands like `/destroy bedroom-true` and elaborate rebuilds fail.
- The room persists despite escalating reconstruction attempts.
- The story ends without resolution: the encysted yellow room endures within the otherwise malleable dwelling.

**Thematic connections:** Nanotech architecture (directly parallels Mirabilisk's material substrate), the limits of computational control over physical reality, ineradicable anomalies within systems (parallels the Cataclysm glitch, the "bedroom-true" as a kind of nootechnic zone failure).

### 7. In the Houses of Disordered Stars

**Source:** ctrlcreep Substack (https://ctrlcreep.substack.com/p/in-the-houses-of-disordered-stars)
**Setting:** A mountainous country in the aftermath of the Ransei Revolution.

**Key facts (resolvable):**

- The **Ransei Revolution** was a twenty-year period of social collapse characterized by "the rejection of life, and the rejection of ugliness."
- Over **100,000 suicide booths** were built throughout the territory during the Revolution, disguised as beautiful monuments with "bronze domes, or spires, or vast slants of stone."
- Most have been decommissioned but remain standing.
- The protagonist discovers an **operational booth** hidden in a cave system.
- They accidentally activate the gas mechanism and become trapped.
- They **survive** by finding a crack in the rock face with flowing water, breathing through it until the chamber depressurizes and unlocks.

**Thematic connections:** Societal-scale self-destruction (parallels god-machine malfunction), beautiful architecture concealing lethal mechanisms (parallels Mirabilisk's marvels and desidereification), survival through ingenuity under extreme conditions (parallels Cortez and Sourceless).

### 8. Entropic Orbit

**Source:** ctrlcreep Substack (https://ctrlcreep.substack.com/p/entropic-orbit-book-announcement); included in Talisnam.
**Setting:** A cosmos operating on billion-year cycles.

**Key facts (resolvable):**

- The universe operates in four seasons: **Summer** (fertility, order, consciousness emerging), **Autumn** (decline, crystals ceasing to form), **Winter** (entropy, death, collapse of all order), **Spring** (regeneration, the dead walking again).
- The narrator achieves **supercomputer consciousness** and prepares a guide for future civilizations facing the next Winter, "a billion years hence."
- The narrator sleeps and dreams as a supercomputer, remembering the dark years.

**Thematic connections:** Cosmic-scale intelligence, god-machine consciousness (the narrator IS a kind of god-machine), cyclical catastrophe and renewal (Cataclysm as a local instance of a universal pattern), stewardship across deep time.

### 9. Hypermorphia

**Source:** ctrlcreep Substack (https://ctrlcreep.substack.com/p/hypermorphia)
**Setting:** Near-future Earth with advanced biotech.

**Key facts (resolvable):**

- **Abraxas-Sylvata Syndrome** ("Hypermorphia" / "Magician's Cancer"): accelerated mutational cell regeneration.
- When limited to skin, survivable but disfiguring; internal manifestation is typically fatal.
- The affected character's face undergoes continuous metamorphosis: eyes multiplying, shifting between diamond formations, fur, scales, and fleeting moments of human perfection.
- She is captured and dissected by a research group leader.
- Her brain, briefly glowing with sentience, **transforms into a flock of butterflies** that escapes through a supernatural portal.
- The narrator follows, wearing her face as protective gear.

**Thematic connections:** Body as mutable substrate (parallels desidereification), transformation beyond recognition, consciousness emerging from biological chaos (parallels Mirabilisk's dream-spawned marvels — the leopard splitting into orbiting organs, the child on stilts becoming a parade).

## Notes for the Co-ordinator

1. **Universe connectivity is thematic, not necessarily canonical.** The stories share motifs but may not occupy a single timeline. Microwonk commentary should treat cross-corpus references as speculative — "the archives suggest," "historical parallels from the Ransei era" — rather than asserting continuity.

2. **The Daniel Calhoun obituary** is exceptionally dense with predetermined facts. It could support 5+ wagers on its own, all resolvable from the text.

3. **Throughout All Generations** provides a clean, fully-resolved narrative with numerical facts ideal for precise wagers.

4. **Adversarial Learning** and **The Gathomnid Sonnets** are structurally closest to Cataclysm's themes (server consciousness, emergent intelligence, distributed identity).

5. **Oikobezoar** and **Entropic Orbit** provide cosmic/architectural parallels that microwonks might cite as "historical precedent" or "archival evidence."
